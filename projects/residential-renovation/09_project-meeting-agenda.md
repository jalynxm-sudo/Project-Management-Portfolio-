# Residential Kitchen Renovation — Project Meeting Agenda

## Meeting Overview

**Project:** Simulated Residential Kitchen Renovation  
**Meeting Type:** Project Planning and Coordination Meeting  
**Meeting Date:** To Be Determined  
**Meeting Duration:** 30 Minutes  
**Meeting Facilitator:** Project Manager  
**Participants:** Homeowner, Project Manager, Contractor  
**Project Status:** Preliminary Planning

## Meeting Purpose

The purpose of this meeting is to review the proposed kitchen renovation plan, confirm stakeholder expectations, discuss preliminary budget and schedule assumptions, identify outstanding decisions, and establish next steps.

This meeting is simulated for portfolio demonstration purposes. It does not represent an actual homeowner or contractor meeting.

## Meeting Objectives

By the end of the meeting, participants should:

1. Review the proposed renovation scope.
2. Identify outstanding homeowner decisions.
3. Discuss preliminary budget assumptions.
4. Review potential schedule constraints.
5. Discuss identified project risks.
6. Confirm responsibilities and next steps.

## Agenda

| Time | Topic | Lead | Objective |

## Discussion Topics

### 1. Project Scope

Review the planned renovation components:

- Demolition and preparation
- Cabinetry and storage
- Countertops
- Flooring
- Plumbing
- Electrical and lighting
- Appliances
- Backsplash and tile
- Painting and finishing
- Installation and general labor

**Discussion Questions:**
- Does the proposed scope reflect the homeowner's current expectations?
- Are any requirements missing?
- Are there areas that require further clarification?

### 2. Design Selections

Review outstanding decisions involving:

- Cabinetry
- Countertop materials
- Flooring
- Backsplash
- Lighting
- Appliances
- Finishes

**Discussion Questions:**
- Which selections still require homeowner approval?
- Are preferred materials available within the preliminary budget?
- Could any selections affect procurement or installation timing?

### 3. Budget Review

Review the preliminary illustrative budget of $42,350.

Discuss:

- Direct project cost assumptions
- Contingency allowance
- Material pricing
- Contractor estimates
- Potential scope changes

The preliminary estimate is not a contractor quote or approved homeowner budget.

**Discussion Questions:**
- Which costs require further validation?
- Are there design choices that could affect the budget?
- What information is needed before the estimate can be refined?

### 4. Schedule Review

Review the preliminary project schedule.

Discuss:

- Design approval
- Contractor coordination
- Material procurement
- Permit and inspection requirements
- Construction sequencing
- Potential schedule dependencies

**Discussion Questions:**
- Are there known timing constraints?
- Which materials may have extended lead times?
- What decisions are needed before construction planning can advance?

### 5. Risk Review

Review the initial project risk register.

Potential concerns include:

- Hidden conditions during demolition
- Material delivery delays
- Budget increases
- Design changes
- Contractor availability
- Permit and inspection requirements
- Safety concerns
- Quality issues

**Discussion Questions:**
- Are there additional risks to document?
- Which risks require further investigation?
- Who will be responsible for monitoring each concern?

### 6. Change Management

Review the proposed change request process.

Discuss how requests will be:

1. Documented
2. Evaluated
3. Reviewed for budget and schedule impact
4. Approved or rejected
5. Incorporated into project documentation when approved

## Decisions Required

The meeting should identify decisions requiring stakeholder review, including:

- Final design selections
- Budget assumptions
- Material preferences
- Schedule constraints
- Contractor coordination
- Outstanding scope questions

Any decisions made during an actual meeting would be documented in the meeting notes.

## Meeting Follow-Up

Following the meeting, the project manager would:

- Document discussion points.
- Record decisions.
- Confirm action-item owners.
- Track outstanding questions.
- Update the project schedule and budget when appropriate.
- Update the risk register and change log as needed.
- Communicate follow-up requirements to stakeholders.

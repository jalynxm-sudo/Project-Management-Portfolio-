# Residential Kitchen Renovation
## Preliminary Project Schedule

**Project Type:** Residential Construction / Kitchen Renovation  
**Project Manager:** Project Manager (Simulated)  
**Project Status:** Planning  
**Project Approach:** Predictive / Waterfall  
**Schedule Type:** Preliminary Planning Estimate

---

## 1. Schedule Purpose

This project schedule organizes the residential kitchen renovation into activities, estimated durations, dependencies, and milestones.

The schedule will support project planning by identifying the sequence of work and establishing a preliminary timeline.

All durations are planning estimates for a simulated project. Actual construction durations would depend on site conditions, contractor availability, material lead times, permits, and approved project scope.

---

## 2. Schedule Assumptions

The preliminary schedule assumes:

- The homeowner participates in requirements gathering and design reviews.
- The existing kitchen layout will be assessed before final design decisions.
- Major design decisions will be made before construction planning is finalized.
- Contractor coordination will be planned before construction begins.
- Material availability will be reviewed before procurement.
- Construction activities will be performed by qualified contractors in a real project.
- The schedule will be revised when actual project information becomes available.

## 4. Project Timeline

### Phase 1 — Initiation and Requirements

**Week 1: Project Initiation**
- Confirm project purpose.
- Identify project stakeholders.
- Establish preliminary project objectives.
- Develop project charter.

**Week 2: Homeowner Requirements**
- Document homeowner needs.
- Identify functional requirements.
- Identify design preferences.
- Establish preliminary project priorities.

**Week 3: Existing Kitchen Assessment**
- Document assumed existing conditions.
- Identify kitchen challenges.
- Identify potential constraints.
- Prepare assessment summary.

### Phase 2 — Design and Planning

**Weeks 4–5: Kitchen Design Development**
- Develop preliminary kitchen layout.
- Plan cabinetry and storage.
- Identify appliance placement.
- Develop material and finish concepts.

**Week 6: Design Review and Approval**
- Review design against homeowner requirements.
- Identify necessary revisions.
- Document design decisions.
- Establish the approved design basis for planning.

**Week 6: Preliminary Budget Development**
- Identify cost categories.
- Develop preliminary cost assumptions.
- Identify contingency requirements.
- Document budget limitations.

### Phase 3 — Coordination and Construction Preparation

**Weeks 7–8: Contractor and Trade Coordination Planning**
- Identify contractor requirements.
- Identify trade coordination needs.
- Review potential construction constraints.
- Document contractor planning assumptions.

**Week 7: Procurement and Material Planning**
- Identify material requirements.
- Review potential supplier lead times.
- Identify procurement dependencies.
- Document material planning risks.

**Week 9: Construction Schedule Development**
- Organize construction activities.
- Identify activity dependencies.
- Estimate construction durations.
- Establish preliminary construction milestones.

### Phase 4 — Simulated Construction

**Weeks 10–13: Simulated Construction Phase**

The simulated construction sequence includes:

1. Site preparation and protection.
2. Demolition planning.
3. Required rough-in work coordination.
4. Flooring and preparation activities.
5. Cabinetry installation planning.
6. Countertop installation planning.
7. Appliance installation coordination.
8. Finishing and final review.

These activities are included to demonstrate construction coordination knowledge. They do not represent actual work performed.

### Phase 5 — Closeout

**Week 14: Final Review and Closeout Planning**
- Review project deliverables.
- Document project status.
- Identify outstanding items.
- Prepare lessons learned.
- Organize portfolio documentation.

## 6. Schedule Dependencies

The preliminary schedule establishes the following relationships:

- Homeowner requirements must be documented before the existing kitchen assessment is finalized.
- The existing kitchen assessment supports design development.
- Design development must be completed before design approval.
- Budget planning depends on preliminary design information.
- Contractor coordination planning depends on the design and budget planning.
- Procurement planning depends on material selections and preliminary budget information.
- Construction schedule development depends on coordination and procurement planning.
- Simulated construction follows construction planning.
- Closeout follows the simulated construction phase.

These dependencies will be reviewed as additional project information becomes available.

---

## 7. Schedule Risks

| Risk | Schedule Impact | Planned Response |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowner decision delays | Design activities may extend | Establish review milestones |
| Material lead times | Procurement and construction delays | Identify lead times early |
| Contractor availability | Coordination delays | Identify resource requirements |
| Scope changes | Additional activities and rework | Document and assess change requests |
| Unforeseen site conditions | Construction schedule changes | Allow for assessment and contingency |
| Permit requirements | Construction start may be delayed | Identify applicable requirements during planning |
